--- Slide 3 ---
Abstract
Smallholder farmers in Karnataka lose crop value to pests, price information asymmetry, low welfare-scheme awareness, and the high upfront cost of farm machinery.
Existing digital agriculture tools solve these problems in isolation, need continuous connectivity, and lack vernacular support.
KrushikaDhara is a Flutter-based, Java/Spring-Boot-backed platform that unifies nine tightly coupled modules under one offline-first architecture for Karnataka farmers.
An INT8-quantised on-device model performs leaf-level pest detection and treatment recommendation with zero server dependency.
A cloud-assisted drone pipeline fuses aerial imagery with Sentinel-2 indices for canopy health and per-acre yield; a P2P rental marketplace uses Bluetooth mesh + geohash discovery for equipment access.
Validated in a six-week, 42-farmer field pilot — results in Section VI — entirely on free-tier infrastructure.

--- Slide 4 ---
Introduction
Agriculture sustains 5M+ farming households across Karnataka's diverse agro-climatic zones — Ragi/Groundnut (Maidan), Coffee/Arecanut (Malnad), Onion/Pomegranate (Dharwad/Bijapur).
A tool built around any single crop context rarely transfers cleanly to another — a recurring obstacle to one product serving the whole state.
Commercial platforms (Kisan Suvidha, AgroStar, DeHaat) each solve fragments: English-first, connectivity-dependent, not tuned to Karnataka-specific diseases, or thin coverage in the south.
KrushikaDhara closes this integration gap rather than adding a tenth isolated app to a farmer's phone.
Built entirely on open-source components and free-tier government + cloud APIs — zero recurring infrastructure cost.

--- Slide 5 ---
Motivation
Pest & Disease Loss: most Karnataka farmers act only after visible crop damage appears, rather than intervening pre-emptively.
Broken Price Discovery: a Kolar tomato grower sells at ₹8–10/kg while the same produce fetches ₹12–14/kg 70 km away at Yeshwanthpur APMC — a 20–30% loss.
Low Scheme Awareness: eligible farmers rarely know of welfare schemes because information sits in dense English/Hindi PDFs, not a queryable form.
Inaccessible Machinery: smallholders can't justify owning a tractor/tiller/sprayer for a 2-acre plot; rental markets stay informal and word-of-mouth.

--- Slide 6 ---
Objectives
Deliver fully offline, on-device pest/disease detection with actionable chemical or organic treatment guidance.
Estimate per-acre yield and map pest hotspots by fusing drone imagery with Sentinel-2 vegetation indices.
Enable a peer-to-peer equipment rental marketplace using geohash discovery with a Bluetooth-mesh offline fallback.
Provide hallucination-free scheme and loan advisory via a Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline grounded in official documents.
Deliver live Mandi price alerts and hyper-local pest-weather risk warnings.
Offer a fully Kannada voice interface so literacy is not a barrier to any module.

--- Slide 7 ---
Social Relevance
Directly targets 5M+ smallholder farming households in Karnataka, many with limited literacy and intermittent connectivity.
Vernacular (Kannada) voice access removes literacy as a barrier to diagnosis, prices, schemes and equipment search.
Zero-cost architecture makes the platform replicable by agricultural universities and NGOs without subscription overhead.
Scheme-RAG module can be deployed as an assisted-digital kiosk inside Raitha Samparka Kendras (RSKs), standardising advisory delivery.
Aggregate disease and yield logs can support DCCB / crop-insurance claim verification and state custom-hiring-centre planning.

--- Slide 8 ---
Literature Review
Mohanty et al. [1]: lab-trained CNN reached 99.35% accuracy on PlantVillage images but dropped to ~31% on real phone photos — no field robustness.
Ferentinos [2]: 99.53% accuracy across 58 crop-disease pairs, but requires uploading every image to a server — not offline.
Akhil et al. [3]: drone-based pest classification for paddy only — single crop, no treatment or yield output.
Geethajali et al. [4]: AI chatbot for yield/crop selection — no offline mode, no vernacular voice.
Rathi et al. [5]: disease detection + price forecasting — no equipment access, no scheme or voice layer.
Patil et al. [6]: web-based equipment rental — no GPS-proximity matching, no offline/mesh fallback.
Gap: no published or commercial system combines offline disease detection, drone yield estimation and vernacular voice in one validated platform.

--- Slide 9 ---
Scope of the Project
Nine loosely-coupled modules covering diagnosis, drone yield analytics, equipment rental, market prices, crop calendar, scheme RAG, pest-weather alerts, loan advisory and voice access.
Targets Karnataka's ten agro-climatic zones; architecture is language- and region-agnostic and can extend to other states with new Bhashini language packs and scheme documents.
Flutter 3.19 client (Android API 24+ / iOS 14+) with a Java 21 / Spring Boot 3.2 backend on Oracle Cloud's Always-Free ARM tier.
Out of scope for this version: in-app escrow payments, IoT soil-sensor ingestion and autonomous drone scheduling — identified as future work.

--- Slide 10 ---
Proposed System
Disease & Pest Detection – TFLite + YOLOv8 (INT8 Quantised) – Offline.
Drone Yield & Health Analysis – Backend CNN + Regression Head + Sentinel-2 NDVI – Online, cached.
Equipment Rental Marketplace – Firebase Realtime DB (Geohash) + Bluetooth Mesh – Hybrid.
Mandi Price Alert – Agmarknet API + FCM – Online.
Crop Calendar – Open-Meteo + Groq Llama 3 – Online.
Scheme RAG – ChromaDB + Llama 3 (LangChain4J) – Online.
Pest-Weather Engine – Open-Meteo + Sentinel-2 NDVI – Online.
Loan Advisory – RAG over DCC Bank / NABARD PDFs – Online.
Voice Pipeline – Bhashini ASR + NMT + TTS – Online.

--- Slide 11 ---
Design and Methodology
Edge Inference: capture → resize to 416×416 → normalise → quantised YOLOv8 TFLite interpreter → confidence gate at 0.5 → disease label + treatment fetched from on-device store. Median latency 178.4 ms, no network call.
Mobile Client: Flutter 3.19, Android API 24+ / iOS 14+, tflite_flutter, camera, geolocator, flutter_nearby_connections; EN/KN via intl.
Java Backend: Spring Boot 3.2 on Java 21 – @Scheduled Agmarknet ingestion, non-blocking WebClient to Open-Meteo/Groq/Bhashini, Apache Tika 2.9 for PDF ingestion.
Vector Store & Drone Model: ChromaDB 0.5.3, 847 chunks, all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384-dim), <40 ms cosine search; feed-forward yield-regression head, retrained quarterly.
All services run on one Oracle Cloud Always-Free ARM instance (2 OCPU / 12 GB RAM), serving ~200 concurrent pilot farmers.

--- Slide 15 ---
Plan for Completion of Project
Federated Learning: aggregate on-device farmer accept/reject signals without sending raw images to a central server, for continuous model improvement.
IoT Soil-Sensor Integration: ingest Bluetooth NPK/pH/moisture sensor data into the crop calendar for plot-specific advice.
Autonomous Drone Scheduling: auto-queue a drone sweep on a high-risk pest-weather convergence.
Escrow & In-App Payments: opt-in payment/deposit-escrow layer for equipment rental.
Cross-Regional Scale-Up: extend beyond Karnataka to Maharashtra / Tamil Nadu with new Bhashini language packs and state scheme documents.
